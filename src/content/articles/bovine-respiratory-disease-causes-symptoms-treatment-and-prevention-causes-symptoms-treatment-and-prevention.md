Bovine respiratory disease (BRD), often called “shipping fever” or bovine respiratory disease complex (BRDC), is the most common and costly disease of feedlot cattle worldwide and a major killer of dairy calves. ## What is bovine respiratory disease causes symptoms treatment and prevention?

Bovine respiratory disease (BRD) is a multifactorial syndrome that affects the lungs and airways of cattle, especially young beef and dairy animals. It is often referred to as bovine respiratory disease complex because several factors must come together: stress, viral infection, and secondary bacterial infection.  When these combine, they overwhelm the calf’s natural defenses and result in pneumonia.

The condition is especially common in recently weaned, transported, or commingled calves entering feedlots, but it also affects dairy calves and sometimes adult cattle. Weaning, long-distance transport, mixing cattle from different sources, and rapid diet changes are major risk times when bovine respiratory disease causes symptoms treatment and prevention become highly relevant for herd management.  

From a veterinary point of view, BRD is not just one disease but a group of respiratory problems with similar signs (fever, coughing, nasal discharge, difficulty breathing) triggered by several pathogens. Key viral agents include bovine herpesvirus-1 (BoHV‑1, IBR virus), bovine respiratory syncytial virus (BRSV), bovine viral diarrhea virus (BVDV), parainfluenza‑3 virus (PI3), and influenza D virus. Major bacterial players include Mannheimia haemolytica, Pasteurella multocida, Histophilus somni, and Mycoplasma bovis.  

Because of this complexity, understanding bovine respiratory disease causes symptoms treatment and prevention requires looking at the animal, the pathogens, and the environment all together rather than focusing on a single cause.

## Causes of bovine respiratory disease causes symptoms treatment and prevention



![Causes of bovine respiratory disease causes symptoms treatment and prevention](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1627328756409-b24f13952074?crop=entropy&cs=tinysrgb&fit=max&fm=jpg&ixid=M3w5ODE1NjV8MHwxfHNlYXJjaHw3fHxib3ZpbmUlMjByZXNwaXJhdG9yeSUyMGRpc2Vhc2UlMjBjYXVzZXMlMjBzeW1wdG9tcyUyMHRyZWF0bWVudCUyMGFuZCUyMHByZXZlbnRpb24lMjBhbmltYWwlMjBkaXNlYXNlfGVufDB8MHx8fDE3ODIyMjEwMjh8MA&ixlib=rb-4.1.0&q=80&w=1080)

When discussing causes of bovine respiratory disease causes symptoms treatment and prevention, it is helpful to think in three layers: stress factors, viral infection, and bacterial overgrowth. Most BRD outbreaks involve all three. 

### 1. Stress and management factors

Stress weakens the immune system, making cattle much more vulnerable to respiratory infections. Important stressors include:

- Weaning, especially abrupt weaning  
- Transport over long distances (“shipping”)  
- Passing through auction markets and commingling with unfamiliar cattle  
- Sudden diet changes to high‑energy rations  
- Dehydration or underfeeding  
- Castration, dehorning, and other surgical procedures done at the wrong time  
- Overcrowding, poor ventilation, dust, and extremes of heat or cold  

Feedlot cattle are at particularly high risk because many of these stresses happen together in a short time. Recently weaned calves may be trucked long distances, mixed with cattle from many sources, processed (vaccination, dehorning, castration), and pushed onto new rations all within days.  

In dairy systems, poor colostrum intake, inadequate housing, poor air quality, and mixing of ages can raise the risk of bovine respiratory disease causes symptoms treatment and prevention, especially in pre‑weaned and weaned calves. 

### 2. Viral pathogens

Respiratory viruses are often the first step in BRD. They damage the lining of the airways, impair the mucociliary “self‑cleaning” system, and suppress local immunity. This makes it easier for bacteria that normally live harmlessly in the nose and throat to move into the lungs and cause pneumonia.  

Important viruses include:

- Bovine herpesvirus 1 (BoHV‑1) – infectious bovine rhinotracheitis (IBR)  
- Bovine respiratory syncytial virus (BRSV)  
- Bovine viral diarrhea virus (BVDV)  
- Parainfluenza‑3 virus (PI3V)  
- Influenza D virus 

### 3. Bacterial pathogens

Once viral damage and stress have weakened defenses, opportunistic bacteria multiply and spread to the lower airways. These are the main cause of severe pneumonia and death in bovine respiratory disease complex.  

Key bacterial agents:

- Mannheimia haemolytica (often the most important in “shipping fever”)  
- Pasteurella multocida  
- Histophilus somni  
- Mycoplasma bovis and related mycoplasmas  

These bacteria can produce powerful toxins and trigger intense inflammation, leading to lung consolidation, fibrinous pneumonia, and even sudden death in severe cases.

## Clinical Signs and Symptoms of bovine respiratory disease causes symptoms treatment and prevention

*Photo: Clinical Signs — via Unsplash*


Recognizing the symptoms of bovine respiratory disease causes symptoms treatment and prevention early is vital. Cattle are prey animals that tend to hide illness, so by the time signs are obvious, lung damage may already be advanced.

### Early and subtle symptoms

Early BRD cases may show only mild or non‑specific signs:

- Slight drop in feed intake or slower to come to feed bunk  
- Reduced activity or separation from herd mates  
- Mild fever (rectal temperature usually > 39.5°C / 103.1°F)  
- Slightly increased respiratory rate or effort  
- Mild, clear nasal discharge  

Close observation, especially during high‑risk periods (first 2–4 weeks after arrival at a feedlot or post‑weaning), is crucial. Automated monitoring of feeding and activity has been shown to help detect respiratory disease in dairy calves earlier than visual checks alone. 

### Obvious clinical signs of pneumonia

As disease progresses, clinical signs of bovine respiratory disease causes symptoms treatment and prevention become clearer:

- Fever (often 40–41°C / 104–105.8°F)  
- Depression, drooped ears, dull eyes, head lowered  
- Marked loss of appetite, weight loss, and poor weight gain  
- Coughing (dry or moist)  
- Nasal discharge that becomes thick, cloudy, or pus‑like  
- Rapid or labored breathing, flared nostrils, open‑mouth breathing in severe cases  
- Increased breathing sounds, crackles, or wheezes on lung auscultation  

In groups, you may also notice higher death loss, more “poor doers,” and a general drop in average daily gain. In feedlot studies, cattle affected with BRD gained about 700 g less per day than unaffected cattle, showing the large economic impact.

## How to Diagnose bovine respiratory disease causes symptoms treatment and prevention

Accurate diagnosis is essential to guide how to treat bovine respiratory disease causes symptoms treatment and prevention and to design long‑term prevention strategies. Diagnosis involves both the individual animal and the herd or group.

### Clinical examination and herd history

Veterinarians usually begin with:

- Detailed history: age, source, transport details, weaning timing, vaccination status, recent stresses, feeding changes, stocking density, and previous disease in the group 
- Physical exam: temperature, respiratory rate and effort, lung and heart sounds, nasal and eye discharge, dehydration, and body condition  

Patterns in the group (how many animals affected, timing after arrival or weaning, and response to earlier treatments) are also important clues in diagnosing bovine respiratory disease causes symptoms treatment and prevention.

### Laboratory tests and imaging

To confirm pathogens and refine treatment and prevention plans, the vet may use:

- Nasal or deep nasopharyngeal swabs for PCR or culture, to identify viruses and bacteria like BoHV‑1, BRSV, BVDV, M. haemolytica, P. multocida, H. somni, and Mycoplasma spp. 
- Transtracheal wash or bronchoalveolar lavage in valuable animals for more accurate lung samples  
- Blood tests (serology) to check exposure to key respiratory viruses and monitor herd immunity 
- Thoracic ultrasound or radiographs in calves to detect lung lesions and assess severity  

Post‑mortem examination of animals that die from suspected BRD can be extremely useful. Lung lesions, culture, and histopathology help identify the main pathogens and inform prevention of bovine respiratory disease causes symptoms treatment and prevention at herd level.

## Treatment of bovine respiratory disease causes symptoms treatment and prevention



![Treatment for bovine respiratory disease causes symptoms treatment and prevention](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1780396493406-a85b7da7cb5f?crop=entropy&cs=tinysrgb&fit=max&fm=jpg&ixid=M3w5ODE1NjV8MHwxfHNlYXJjaHw0OXx8dmV0ZXJpbmFyaWFuJTIwZmFybWVyJTIwdHJlYXRtZW50fGVufDB8MHx8fDE3ODIyMDU5ODd8MA&ixlib=rb-4.1.0&q=80&w=1080)


*Photo: Treatment — via Unsplash*


How to treat bovine respiratory disease causes symptoms treatment and prevention depends on early detection, correct antibiotic choice, supportive care, and evaluation of herd‑level risk. Early, aggressive treatment generally gives the best chance of full recovery and minimal lung scarring. 

### Antimicrobial (antibiotic) therapy

Broad‑spectrum injectable antimicrobials labeled for BRD are the mainstay of treatment. Common first‑line drugs (used according to veterinary guidance and local regulations) include:

- Long‑acting macrolides such as tulathromycin and gamithromycin  
- Florfenicol (a phenicol antibiotic)  
- Other labeled options such as oxytetracycline and related medications  

These drugs are chosen because they reach high concentrations in lung tissue and have sustained action after a single injection, which is practical in feedlot and herd situations. For example, tulathromycin (a macrolide) and florfenicol are widely used to treat and control BRD in beef and non‑lactating dairy cattle.  

Antibiotic selection should be guided by:

- Veterinary diagnosis and herd treatment protocols  
- Likely pathogens in your region and operation  
- Previous response to therapy  
- Culture and sensitivity testing where available  
- Responsible antimicrobial stewardship to reduce resistance 

### Supportive care and nursing

Alongside antibiotics, supportive care improves outcomes:

- Provide easy access to clean water and palatable feed; separate sick animals so they are not bullied away from feed bunks.  
- Reduce stress: handle cattle quietly, avoid unnecessary movements, and protect them from extreme weather.  
- Ensure good bedding and dry lying areas, particularly for dairy calves.  
- Non‑steroidal anti‑inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs), prescribed by a vet, may be used to control fever and pain, improving appetite and comfort.  

Severely affected calves, especially dairy calves, may require more intensive treatment, including oral or intravenous fluids and oxygen therapy where facilities allow. 

### Metaphylaxis in high‑risk groups

In some feedlot or high‑risk dairy calf situations, metaphylaxis (group treatment) may be recommended. This involves administering a long‑acting antimicrobial to an entire group of cattle at high risk of developing BRD (for example, light‑weight, high‑risk auction calves) at arrival, even if they are not yet showing clear clinical signs.  

Metaphylaxis has been shown to significantly reduce BRD incidence and mortality in feedlot cattle but must be used judiciously under veterinary guidance to balance disease control against antimicrobial resistance concerns.

## Prevention and Control of bovine respiratory disease causes symptoms treatment and prevention



![Prevention of bovine respiratory disease causes symptoms treatment and prevention](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1504867991871-57c29096e624?crop=entropy&cs=tinysrgb&fit=max&fm=jpg&ixid=M3w5ODE1NjV8MHwxfHNlYXJjaHwyOHx8ZmFybSUyMGJpb3NlY3VyaXR5JTIwcHJldmVudGlvbiUyMGFuaW1hbHxlbnwwfDB8fHwxNzgyMjIxMDI3fDA&ixlib=rb-4.1.0&q=80&w=1080)


*Photo: Prevention — via Unsplash*


Prevention of bovine respiratory disease causes symptoms treatment and prevention is more effective and economical than treatment alone. Because BRD is multifactorial, prevention programs must address colostrum management, housing, stress reduction, vaccination, biosecurity, and strategic use of metaphylaxis where appropriate. 

### Management and stress reduction (husbandry)

Key management strategies include:

- **Preconditioning programs**: wean calves 2–3 weeks before shipment, introduce them to feed bunks and water troughs, perform castration/dehorning well before transport, and keep them on a stable ration. These preconditioned calves have lower BRD risk. 
- **Weaning management**: avoid abrupt weaning plus transport plus surgery all on the same day. Spread stressors out in time when possible. 
- **Transport and commingling**: minimize journey times, avoid overcrowding in trucks, and reduce mixing of cattle from multiple sources.  
- **Housing and ventilation**: in calves and housed cattle, ensure good air quality, low humidity, and reduced dust. Avoid overcrowding and mixing of age groups. 
- **Nutrition**: introduce high‑energy rations gradually, correct vitamin and mineral deficiencies, and avoid acidosis and indigestion, which can impair immunity. 

### Vaccination programs

Vaccination is a cornerstone of prevention of bovine respiratory disease causes symptoms treatment and prevention. Well‑designed vaccine protocols can reduce the severity and incidence of BRD by priming the immune system against major viral and some bacterial pathogens.  

Common vaccine targets include:

- Viruses: BoHV‑1 (IBR), BVDV, BRSV, PI3  
- Bacteria: Mannheimia haemolytica and sometimes Histophilus somni or Pasteurella multocida  

Important principles:

- **Timing**: Vaccination is more effective when done as part of preconditioning, 2–4 weeks before high‑risk events such as weaning or feedlot entry, rather than on the day of arrival. 
- **Boosters**: Many vaccines require two doses 2–4 weeks apart to achieve full protection (for example, some combined viral–bacterial products). 
- **Herd‑specific plans**: Work with your veterinarian to tailor vaccine choices and schedules to your production system (cow‑calf, dairy, backgrounding, feedlot) and local pathogen patterns. 

### Biosecurity and herd health planning

Long‑term control of bovine respiratory disease causes symptoms treatment and prevention also depends on:

- Limiting introduction of new animals without proper quarantine and health checks.  
- Separating sick calves and managing them last to reduce spread of pathogens.  
- All‑in/all‑out systems where feasible to allow thorough cleaning and disinfection between groups.  
- Routine monitoring for early detection (daily checks, scoring systems, or automated behavior monitoring).  

Integrating all these measures into a written herd health plan, regularly reviewed with your veterinarian, provides the strongest defense against BRD.

## Frequently Asked Questions About bovine respiratory disease causes symptoms treatment and prevention

### Q: What are the first signs of bovine respiratory disease in calves?

**A:** Early symptoms of bovine respiratory disease causes symptoms treatment and prevention include mild fever, reduced appetite, slower approach to the feed bunk, slight depression, and subtle increases in breathing rate. You may notice a calf standing apart from the group with drooped ears and a small amount of clear nasal discharge before obvious coughing starts. 

### Q: How do I treat bovine respiratory disease causes symptoms treatment and prevention on my farm?

**A:** How to treat bovine respiratory disease causes symptoms treatment and prevention starts with early detection and veterinary guidance. A vet will usually prescribe an injectable antibiotic labeled for BRD (such as a long‑acting macrolide or florfenicol) and may add anti‑inflammatory medications to reduce fever and pain. Supportive care—good nutrition, water, protection from weather, and reduced stress—is also essential. Severely affected calves may need more intensive care or hospitalization. 

### Q: Can humans get bovine respiratory disease?

**A:** The specific viruses and bacteria that cause bovine respiratory disease in cattle are adapted to cattle and are not considered typical causes of respiratory disease in humans. However, people can carry these organisms between groups of cattle and may be exposed to other zoonotic agents in farm environments. Good hygiene (handwashing, clean clothing and boots, and proper handling of sick animals) is always recommended, but BRD itself is not generally viewed as a direct human health disease. 

### Q: How can I prevent bovine respiratory disease in newly arrived feedlot calves?

**A:** Prevention of bovine respiratory disease causes symptoms treatment and prevention in feedlot calves centers on buying preconditioned animals when possible, reducing transport and handling stress, implementing well‑timed vaccination programs before arrival, and considering metaphylaxis for high‑risk calves under veterinary advice. Good bunk management, gradual ration changes, dust control, and close monitoring during the first month after arrival are also crucial. 

### Q: What are the long‑term effects of bovine respiratory disease on performance?

**A:** Even if affected cattle survive, bovine respiratory disease often causes permanent lung damage and reduced growth. Studies show that cattle which had BRD can have significantly lower average daily gain compared with unaffected herd mates and may finish later or at lighter weights, reducing profitability. Some may remain chronic “poor doers,” and severe early‑life pneumonia in dairy calves can reduce future milk production. 

### Q: Do vaccines completely stop bovine respiratory disease?

**A:** Vaccines are a vital part of prevention of bovine respiratory disease causes symptoms treatment and prevention but do not guarantee that no animal will ever get sick. Instead, good vaccination programs reduce the number of cases, lessen disease severity, and improve survival. Their success depends on correct timing, proper handling and administration, and integration with good management and biosecurity practices.

## When to Call a Veterinarian

Prompt veterinary involvement greatly improves outcomes and helps control costs associated with bovine respiratory disease causes symptoms treatment and prevention. Contact a veterinarian if you notice:

- Multiple calves or feedlot cattle with fever, coughing, nasal discharge, or increased breathing effort, especially within days to weeks of weaning or arrival.  
- Individual animals with severe depression, open‑mouth breathing, or failure to respond to an initial treatment.  
- A pattern of recurring respiratory disease in certain age groups or pens, suggesting a larger management or pathogen issue.  

Veterinarians can:

- Confirm the diagnosis and rule out other causes of respiratory signs.  
- Recommend appropriate antibiotics and treatment protocols, including withdrawal times.  
- Collect samples for lab testing to pinpoint specific pathogens.  
- Design or update vaccination and prevention programs tailored to your farm.  
- Help implement responsible antimicrobial use and monitor treatment success over time.  

Early professional help is especially important in dairy calves and high‑value breeding or show animals, where the long‑term impact of lung damage can be severe.

Bovine respiratory disease causes symptoms treatment and prevention are central concerns for modern cattle production. Understanding the causes of bovine respiratory disease causes symptoms treatment and prevention—particularly the roles of weaning, transport, housing, nutrition, and pathogen exposure—allows farmers and veterinarians to target weak points in management. With careful planning and close collaboration with your veterinarian, it is possible to significantly lower the burden of BRD, improve animal welfare, and protect the profitability of both beef and dairy operations.
