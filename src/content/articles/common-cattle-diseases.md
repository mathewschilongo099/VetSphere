# Common Diseases in Cattle and Their Prevention

Cattle diseases are a significant concern for farmers and veterinarians across Africa. Understanding common diseases and implementing prevention strategies is crucial for maintaining a healthy and productive herd.

## Introduction

Cattle disease management starts with knowledge. Whether you're raising dairy or beef cattle, understanding common health threats and implementing preventive measures will improve your herd's overall productivity and profitability.

## Major Cattle Diseases

### Brucellosis

Brucellosis is a contagious bacterial disease that affects cattle reproduction, causing abortion, reduced milk production, and infertility.

**Prevention:**
- Vaccinate young heifers with strain 19 vaccine
- Test and remove positive animals
- Practice proper farm hygiene
- Separate infected animals immediately

### Bovine Tuberculosis

This chronic disease spreads through respiratory droplets and can affect multiple organs, particularly the lungs and lymph nodes.

**Prevention:**
- Regular tuberculin testing
- Quarantine new animals before introducing to herd
- Maintain good ventilation in housing
- Separate infected animals

### Foot-and-Mouth Disease (FMD)

An extremely contagious viral disease causing blisters in the mouth, teats, and feet, leading to lameness and reduced productivity.

**Prevention:**
- Implement strict biosecurity measures
- Regular vaccination as per government guidelines
- Quarantine diseased animals
- Disinfect equipment and vehicles

### Bovine Viral Diarrhea (BVD)

This viral disease causes diarrhea, immune suppression, and reproductive issues in infected cattle.

**Prevention:**
- Vaccination programs for breeding stock
- Regular testing and removal of persistently infected animals
- Proper colostrum management
- Good hygiene practices

## Prevention Strategies

### Biosecurity Measures

1. **Farm Entry Control**
   - Designate entry/exit points
   - Disinfect vehicles entering the farm
   - Limit visitor access to productive areas

2. **Feed and Water Management**
   - Use clean, uncontaminated feed sources
   - Ensure fresh water supply
   - Prevent water stagnation

3. **Animal Segregation**
   - Keep sick animals separated
   - Isolate new animals for 30 days
   - Maintain age and production-stage groupings

### Nutritional Management

- **Balanced Diet**: Ensure adequate energy, protein, minerals, and vitamins
- **Mineral Supplementation**: Particularly zinc, copper, and selenium
- **Clean Feed**: Store feed in rodent-proof containers
- **Quality Hay**: Test hay for mold contamination

## Vaccination Programs

### Core Vaccines (Recommended for all cattle)

| Disease | Age | Frequency |
|---------|-----|-----------|
| Brucellosis | 3-12 months (females) | Once |
| Blackleg | 4-12 weeks | Booster 3-4 weeks later |
| Botulism | 3-4 weeks | Annual |
| BVD | Before breeding | Follow label directions |

### Risk-Based Vaccines

- Anthrax
- Leptospirosis
- Clostridial diseases
- Respiratory viruses

## Management Practices

### Housing and Sanitation

- Maintain clean, dry bedding
- Ensure adequate ventilation
- Remove manure regularly
- Disinfect equipment

### Health Monitoring

- Regular visual inspection
- Temperature monitoring for sick animals
- Record keeping for health events
- Early treatment intervention

### Stress Reduction

- Minimize transport stress
- Avoid overcrowding
- Provide shade and shelter
- Handle animals calmly

## When to Call a Veterinarian

Contact your veterinarian immediately if you notice:

✓ Sudden illness or death
✓ Persistent diarrhea or constipation
✓ Lameness or swelling
✓ Abnormal behavior or neurological signs
✓ Reproductive problems (abortion, retained placenta)
✓ Eye or respiratory symptoms
✓ Signs of pneumonia or septicemia

## Conclusion

Implementing a comprehensive disease prevention program requires dedication to biosecurity, vaccination, nutrition, and good management practices. Regular consultation with your veterinarian ensures your herd remains healthy, productive, and profitable.
