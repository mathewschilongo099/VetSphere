Why Small Dogs Are More Vulnerable to Parasites
Small dog breeds — Chihuahuas, Shih Tzus, Pomeranians, Dachshunds — have less body mass, which means flea infestations and tick-borne illnesses hit them harder and faster than larger dogs. A heavy flea burden can cause anemia in a small dog within days.
Common threats include:
Fleas — cause intense itching, skin infections, tapeworms, and anemia
Ticks — transmit Lyme disease, Rocky Mountain Spotted Fever, and Ehrlichiosis
Mites — often spread alongside flea infestations
Prevention is always safer and cheaper than treatment.

Fleas vs. Ticks: Know Your Enemy
Fleas
Fleas are the most common external parasite in dogs worldwide. A single female flea lays up to 50 eggs per day. An infestation can develop in your home in under two weeks. Signs your dog has fleas:
Constant scratching, especially around the tail base and belly
Small black specks ("flea dirt") in the fur
Restless, irritable behavior
Pale gums (in severe infestations — sign of anemia)
Ticks
Ticks attach to your dog's skin and feed on blood for days. They're most dangerous because of the diseases they transmit. Check your dog for ticks after every outdoor walk, especially in grassy or wooded areas. Signs of tick-borne illness include:
Lethargy and loss of appetite
Fever
Swollen joints or limping
Swollen lymph nodes
A collar provides barrier protection that repels and kills both before they can attach or lay eggs.
