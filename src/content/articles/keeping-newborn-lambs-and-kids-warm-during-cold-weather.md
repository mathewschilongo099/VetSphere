When a cold snap hits your lambing or kidding season, the temperature in the paddock or shed drops faster than a newborn can adjust. During cold weather, lambs are able to maintain normal body temperature for only a few hours after birth before their internal systems begin to struggle. If you are not watching closely, that tiny window closes before you even realize the animal is in trouble.

## Catching the drop in body heat early

Neonatal hypothermia is defined as a body temperature drop below 39 degrees Celsius (102.2 degrees Fahrenheit) in newborn lambs and kids. When a newborn gets too cold, it stops searching for the udder, hunches its back, and becomes sluggish. You might find it tucked away in a corner or lying flat out instead of bouncing around the pen. Checking the internal temperature with a thermometer tells you immediately if the animal has slipped past its normal baseline. Chilling is a common and preventable cause of young animal deaths, making early detection your best tool for keeping mortality rates down.

## How brown fat burns to keep newborns alive

![a small white lamb standing in a green grass field](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1711648988212-0ad688a70e29?crop=entropy&cs=tinysrgb&fit=max&fm=jpg&ixid=M3w5ODE1NjV8MHwxfHNlYXJjaHwyfHxsYW1icyUyMG9uJTIwYSUyMGZhcm18ZW58MXwwfHx8MTc5NTcyNTMwN3ww&ixlib=rb-4.1.0&q=80&w=1080)


Unlike older animals that rely on shivering to warm up, newborn lambs and kids rely primarily on non-shivering thermogenesis driven by the metabolism of brown adipose tissue (BAT). This specialized brown fat is stored inside the body specifically to generate rapid heat when the animal enters the cold outside world. The oxidation of this brown fat burns through energy at a high rate to maintain core body temperature. However, that internal heater has a strict time limit. Once the brown fat reserves run dry, the newborn cannot generate its own heat anymore unless it gets a fresh influx of energy from milk.

## Why a chilled gut blocks colostrum absorption

![An adult sheep and a lamb standing in a grassy field.](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1627730433054-9ccb05ef8c6a?crop=entropy&cs=tinysrgb&fit=max&fm=jpg&ixid=M3w5ODE1NjV8MHwxfHNlYXJjaHwzfHxld2VzJTIwYW5kJTIwbGFtYnN8ZW58MXwwfHx8MTc5NTcyNTMwOXww&ixlib=rb-4.1.0&q=80&w=1080)


When hypothermia sets in, the damage goes far beyond a drop in temperature. Hypothermia inhibits the absorption of immune components from colostrum which protect the lamb from infectious pathogens. The gut lining shuts down its ability to pass those vital antibodies into the bloodstream. Even if the newborn manages to swallow milk later, a cold gut cannot process the protection it needs to fight off disease. This leaves the animal wide open to secondary infections that strike down weakened stock.

SDSU Extension estimates that in the U.S., lamb mortality from all causes is approximately 20% with more than 80% of those losses occurring in the first two-weeks following lambing. When you look at the top causes for newborn lamb losses, starvation, hypothermia, cold stress, respiratory disease, and scours followed by injury top the list. These issues often chain together. A cold lamb stops nursing, becomes starved and hypothermic, loses its immune protection, and finally succumbs to scours or respiratory illness.

## Managing ewes and nannies for vigorous offspring

Preventing cold stress starts long before the birth happens. Make sure ewes and nannies are maintained in excellent health so offspring are born stress-free, good sized, and vigorous with adequate colostrum. Well-nourished mothers produce thick, nutrient-dense colostrum and have the stamina to clean and care for their young immediately after birth. A vigorous newborn stands up quickly, finds the teat without help, and gets that crucial first meal into its stomach before the cold can sap its strength.

Make sure lambs and kids nurse quickly after birth to fuel their metabolic need and maintain body heat. If a mother lacks condition, her milk yield suffers, and her offspring are born weak with very little reserve energy to fight off low ambient temperatures. Local agro-dealers often stock supplementary feeds and mineral licks that help keep pregnant stock in solid condition through the final trimester.

Science does not yet have all the answers mapped out for every situation. Specifically, there are no universally agreed protocols or step-by-step rewarming procedures for severely hypothermic lambs and kids that guarantee recovery. Because these methods lack standardized guidelines, you should ask a veterinarian or your local veterinary authority for the safest approach when you encounter a severely chilled animal that fails to respond to basic warming.

## Jobs for the lambing shed this week

Walk your pens daily to check for drafts and patch up any gaps in the walls where wind can cut through. Lay down fresh, dry straw or bedding so newborns do not drop onto cold, damp earth. Keep your colostrum collection supplies clean and easily accessible near the pens so you can act instantly when a struggling newborn needs a jump start.
