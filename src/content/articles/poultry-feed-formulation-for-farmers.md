Poultry feed formulation is one of the most important skills for profitable chicken farming. Feed affects growth rate, egg production, immunity, body weight, eggshell quality, mortality, and the overall cost of production.

A cheap feed is not always an economical feed. If it lacks protein, energy, amino acids, calcium, or vitamins, your birds may grow slowly, lay fewer eggs, develop weak bones, or become more vulnerable to disease. A properly formulated ration gives your flock the nutrients it needs at the right age and production stage.

This guide explains the basics of poultry feed formulation for farmers, including ingredients, nutrient requirements, feed calculations, mixing methods, storage, and common mistakes.

## What Is Poultry Feed Formulation?

Poultry feed formulation is the process of combining different feed ingredients in specific proportions to produce a balanced diet for chickens.

The formula should match:

- The type of bird
- The age of the bird
- The production purpose
- The expected feed intake
- The nutrient content of available ingredients
- The local cost and availability of ingredients

Broilers need feed designed for rapid muscle growth. Layers need a diet that supports egg production and eggshell formation. Chicks require highly digestible feed with sufficient protein, amino acids, vitamins, and minerals.

Poultry require many nutrients in the correct balance. Energy, protein, amino acids, calcium, phosphorus, sodium, vitamins, trace minerals, fats, and water all contribute to flock performance. A diet that contains enough crude protein may still be deficient in essential amino acids such as lysine or methionine. 

## Why Proper Feed Formulation Matters

Feed is usually one of the largest expenses on a poultry farm. Formulating it accurately helps you control costs without sacrificing bird health or performance.

A balanced feed can help you:

- Achieve better weight gain in broilers
- Improve feed conversion
- Increase egg production
- Maintain strong eggshells
- Reduce nutritional diseases
- Improve feathering and body condition
- Reduce feed wastage
- Use safe, locally available ingredients
- Maintain consistent flock performance

An unbalanced ration can produce poor growth, thin eggshells, leg problems, excessive fat, diarrhea, reduced fertility, or increased mortality. Improper mixing can also create areas of excessive medication, salt, vitamins, or minerals, which may harm birds. 

## First Identify the Type and Age of Your Birds

Never use one poultry feed formula for every bird on your farm. Nutrient requirements change significantly as birds grow.

### Broiler feed stages

Broilers are commonly fed in phases:

- **Broiler starter:** Approximately 0–3 or 0–4 weeks
- **Broiler grower:** Approximately 3–6 or 4–8 weeks
- **Broiler finisher:** From about 6–8 weeks until market

Typical crude protein targets are approximately:

- Starter: 21–23%
- Grower: About 19–20%
- Finisher: About 17–18%

The Merck Veterinary Manual lists typical broiler crude protein requirements of 23% during 0–3 weeks, 20% during 3–6 weeks, and 18% during 6–8 weeks when diets contain approximately 3,200 kcal of metabolizable energy per kilogram.  

Protein levels normally decrease as broilers age, while energy remains important for efficient weight gain. However, the exact formula should be adjusted for genetics, climate, feed intake, target market weight, and the recommendations of your breeding company.

### Layer feed stages

Layer chickens usually receive:

- Chick starter
- Pullet grower
- Developer or pre-layer feed
- Layer feed

Growing pullets need adequate protein for body and skeletal development, but they should not receive a high-calcium layer ration too early. Feeding layer diets to nonlaying birds can cause kidney damage and poor bone development because of the high calcium concentration.  

Once hens begin laying, calcium becomes a major priority. Layer diets commonly contain approximately 15–19% crude protein and around 3–4% calcium, depending on feed intake, breed, age, and egg production level. 

## The Main Nutrients in Poultry Feed

### 1. Energy

Energy fuels movement, body maintenance, growth, egg production, and temperature regulation.

Common energy ingredients include:

- Maize or corn
- Wheat
- Sorghum
- Millet
- Barley
- Cassava meal
- Rice bran
- Vegetable oil
- Animal fat, where legally permitted and appropriate

Grains normally provide most of the energy in a poultry ration. Oils and fats are concentrated energy sources and can increase feed energy, reduce dust, and improve palatability.

Too little dietary energy may cause poor growth and excessive feed consumption. Too much energy may reduce feed intake or encourage excessive fat deposition.

### 2. Protein and amino acids

Protein supports muscle growth, tissue repair, feather development, egg production, and enzymes.

Protein ingredients may include:

- Soybean meal
- Sunflower meal
- Canola meal
- Groundnut cake
- Cottonseed meal
- Fish meal
- Meat and bone meal
- Insect meal
- Dried legume products

Crude protein alone does not tell you whether a ration is fully balanced. Poultry also need essential amino acids, particularly lysine, methionine, threonine, tryptophan, and arginine.

For example, a diet may meet its crude protein target but still produce poor growth if lysine or methionine is inadequate. Synthetic amino acids can sometimes allow you to lower total crude protein while maintaining performance, but they must be weighed and mixed carefully.

### 3. Calcium and phosphorus

Calcium is essential for:

- Eggshel formation
- Bone strength
- Muscle function
- Nerve function
- Blood clotting

Common calcium sources include:

- Limestone
- Oyster shell
- Calcite
- Shell grit

Phosphorus supports bone formation, energy metabolism, and cellular function. Sources include:

- Dicalcium phosphate
- Monocalcium phosphate
- Meat and bone meal
- Some plant ingredients

Do not calculate phosphorus using total phosphorus alone. Much of the phosphorus in plant ingredients is bound in phytate and is not fully available to poultry. Available phosphorus should be considered during formulation. Calcium must also be balanced with available phosphorus; for growing birds, a calcium-to-available-phosphorus ratio close to 2:1 is commonly recommended.  

Laying hens may benefit from coarse limestone or oyster shell because larger particles remain in the digestive tract longer and can supply calcium during shell formation, including overnight. 

### 4. Vitamins and trace minerals

Vitamins and trace minerals are required in small quantities but have major effects on health and production.

Important nutrients include:

- Vitamins A, D, E, and K
- B-complex vitamins
- Choline
- Selenium
- Zinc
- Manganese
- Copper
- Iron
- Iodine

Vitamin D helps birds use calcium and phosphorus. Manganese and zinc support bone development, feathering, immunity, and eggshell quality.

Use a commercial poultry vitamin-mineral premix designed for the specific bird type. Follow the manufacturer’s inclusion rate exactly. Premixes may be highly concentrated, so small weighing errors can create deficiencies or toxicity.

### 5. Salt and electrolytes

Salt supplies sodium and chloride, which help regulate fluid balance, nerve function, and digestion.

Too little salt can reduce appetite and growth. Too much can cause excessive drinking, wet litter, salt poisoning, and death. Never estimate salt by eye. Use a reliable scale and follow a professional formula.

### 6. Water

Water is not technically a feed ingredient, but it is the most important nutrient. Birds should have continuous access to clean, fresh water.

Poor water quality can reduce feed intake and cause digestive problems. Clean drinkers regularly, prevent algae growth, and check that all birds can reach the water.

## Common Poultry Feed Ingredients

A practical poultry ration normally contains several ingredient groups.

### Energy ingredients

- Maize
- Wheat
- Sorghum
- Millet
- Cassava meal
- Rice products

### Protein ingredients

- Soybean meal
- Fish meal
- Sunflower meal
- Groundnut cake
- Canola meal
- Insect meal
- Legume meals

### Fiber and bulk ingredients

- Wheat bran
- Rice bran
- Maize bran
- Wheat middlings

These ingredients should be used carefully because excessive fiber can reduce energy density and digestibility.

### Mineral ingredients

- Limestone
- Oyster shell
- Dicalcium phosphate
- Monocalcium phosphate
- Salt

### Special additives

Depending on local regulations and professional advice, poultry diets may contain:

- Vitamin-mineral premixes
- Enzymes such as phytase
- Antioxidants
- Probiotics
- Organic acids
- Approved coccidiostats
- Mold inhibitors

Do not add antibiotics, medications, growth promoters, or toxin binders without checking local regulations, withdrawal requirements, and veterinary guidance.

## How to Formulate Poultry Feed Step by Step

### Step 1: Set the nutrient targets

Decide whether you are formulating broiler starter, broiler finisher, pullet grower, or layer feed.

Write down your target levels for:

- Metabolizable energy
- Crude protein
- Lysine
- Methionine
- Threonine
- Calcium
- Available phosphorus
- Sodium
- Vitamin and mineral premix

Use current recommendations for your breed and production system. Published nutrient tables are useful starting points, but modern commercial strains may have requirements that differ from older minimum recommendations. 

### Step 2: Analyze your ingredients

Find the nutrient composition of every ingredient from:

- A laboratory analysis
- A trusted feed supplier
- A reputable feed table
- A consistent manufacturer specification

Ingredient quality varies. Maize grown in different locations may have different moisture, energy, protein, and toxin levels. Do not assume that every batch has the same nutritional value.

### Step 3: Select ingredients

Choose ingredients based on:

- Nutrient value
- Price
- Availability
- Digestibility
- Safety
- Storage life
- Compatibility with other ingredients

The least expensive ingredient is not always the cheapest source of nutrients. A low-cost ingredient with poor digestibility, high moisture, or contamination may reduce performance and increase losses.

### Step 4: Balance the major ingredients

Include an energy source, protein sources, minerals, salt, and premix. Then balance amino acids, calcium, available phosphorus, and energy.

A simple Pearson Square can help estimate the amount of two ingredients needed to reach a crude protein target. The method involves placing the protein levels of two ingredients on opposite corners, placing the desired protein percentage in the center, subtracting diagonally, and converting the resulting parts into percentages. It is useful for simple two-ingredient calculations but does not balance energy, amino acids, minerals, or cost at the same time. 

### Step 5: Add the premix and micro-ingredients

Do not pour tiny quantities directly into a large batch. First mix the premix, salt, amino acids, and other micro-ingredients with a small quantity of bran or another carrier. This creates a premix that can be distributed more evenly.

Poor distribution of micro-ingredients can result in some birds receiving too much while others receive too little.

### Step 6: Mix thoroughly

A mechanical mixer is preferred for larger quantities. Add ingredients in a logical sequence:

1. Add part of the grain component.
2. Add protein meals.
3. Add mineral ingredients.
4. Add the premix or micro-ingredient carrier.
5. Add the remaining grain.
6. Mix for the recommended time.

If adding oil, apply it gradually while the mixer is operating. Excess liquid can cause clumping and uneven distribution.

### Step 7: Check the finished feed

Inspect the final feed for:

- Uniform color and texture
- Clumps
- Unmixed mineral pockets
- Off odors
- Mold
- Excessive dust
- Unusual moisture

For commercial production or high-value flocks, send feed samples to a laboratory for protein, moisture, calcium, phosphorus, salt, and mycotoxin testing.

## Example Feed Formulation for Farmers

The following is an educational example, not a universal formula:

### Example broiler grower feed per 100 kg

- Maize: 58 kg
- Soybean meal: 27 kg
- Wheat bran: 7 kg
- Vegetable oil: 3 kg
- Fish meal or another approved protein source: 2 kg
- Dicalcium phosphate: 1.2 kg
- Limestone: 1.1 kg
- Salt: 0.3 kg
- Vitamin-mineral premix: 0.4 kg

Total: 100 kg

This example must be recalculated using the actual nutrient values of your ingredients. The formula may need changes in soybean meal, oil, minerals, amino acids, or premix to meet the target energy and amino acid levels.

Do not copy a feed formula blindly. A formula that works in one country or season may be unsuitable when ingredient quality, breed, climate, or premix concentration changes.

## Feed Safety and Storage

Never use rotten, moldy, insect-damaged, or foul-smelling ingredients. Mycotoxins can occur in maize, groundnuts, oilseed meals, and other ingredients. These toxins may reduce growth, weaken immunity, lower egg production, and affect food safety.  

Important storage practices include:

- Store feed in a cool, dry, well-ventilated room.
- Keep bags off the floor on pallets.
- Protect feed from rain, condensation, rodents, and insects.
- Use older feed first.
- Keep storage areas clean.
- Do not mix new feed with spoiled residue.
- Clean feeders and bins regularly.
- Avoid storing feed near chemicals, fuel, or pesticides.
- Buy quantities that your flock can consume while fresh.

Visible mold is a warning sign, but the absence of visible mold does not prove that feed is free of mycotoxins. Some toxins remain after mold disappears and may not be destroyed by ordinary feed processing. 

## Common Feed Formulation Mistakes

Avoid these mistakes:

### Feeding the wrong diet to the wrong birds

Do not feed layer diets to chicks or pullets. Do not feed chick starter to adult layers for long periods.

### Adding too much scratch grain

Grains can dilute protein, vitamins, minerals, and amino acids in a complete ration. If you offer scratch grain, provide it sparingly and account for its nutrients.

### Guessing premix quantities

A small error in a concentrated premix can seriously affect the flock. Always use an accurate scale.

### Ignoring feed intake

Nutrient concentration depends partly on how much birds eat. A diet formulated for 110 grams of daily feed intake may not meet requirements if birds consume much less.

### Using spoiled ingredients

Moldy grain should not be used simply because it is cheap. Contaminated ingredients can cause greater financial losses than the cost of replacement.

### Changing formulas suddenly

Sudden feed changes can reduce intake and cause digestive upset. Introduce a new ration gradually over several days.

### Ignoring feed wastage

Wet litter, overflowing feeders, poor feeder height, and rodents can increase feed costs substantially. Adjust feeders so birds can eat comfortably without throwing feed onto the floor.

## When to Buy Commercial Feed

Homemade poultry feed can be useful when ingredients are reliable and you have accurate scales, proper storage, and formulation support.

Commercial feed may be safer when:

- You keep a small flock.
- You cannot test ingredients.
- You lack mixing equipment.
- You are unfamiliar with amino acid balancing.
- You are raising high-performance broilers.
- You need medicated feed with precise withdrawal requirements.
- Ingredient quality changes frequently.

Complete commercial feed offers convenience and more consistent nutrient distribution. It is especially useful for chicks and young birds, which are less tolerant of nutritional errors. 

## Frequently Asked Questions

### 1. What is the best ingredient for poultry feed?

There is no single best ingredient. Maize or sorghum can supply energy, soybean meal can provide protein, and limestone can provide calcium, but birds need a balanced combination of ingredients. The best formula depends on nutrient quality, price, availability, and bird age.

### 2. Can I feed maize alone to chickens?

No. Maize supplies energy but is low in several essential amino acids, vitamins, and minerals. Feeding maize alone can cause poor growth, low egg production, weak bones, and nutrient deficiencies.

### 3. How much protein does broiler feed need?

A common guide is approximately 21–23% protein for starter feed, 19–20% for grower feed, and 17–18% for finisher feed. Exact levels should be adjusted for strain, energy density, climate, and production goals. 

### 4. How much calcium does layer feed need?

Many layer diets contain approximately 3–4% calcium, depending on feed intake, breed, age, and egg production. Calcium should be balanced with available phosphorus and supported by adequate vitamin D.

### 5. Can pullets eat layer feed?

It is best to avoid feeding layer feed to growing pullets. Its high calcium level can damage developing kidneys and bones. Use a pullet grower or developer ration until birds approach the laying stage. 

### 6. Is fish meal necessary in poultry feed?

No. Fish meal is one possible protein source, but it can be replaced partly or completely by soybean meal, sunflower meal, canola meal, insect meal, or other suitable ingredients. The final diet must still meet amino acid requirements.

### 7. How long can poultry feed be stored?

Storage life depends on moisture, temperature, fat content, packaging, and ingredient quality. Store feed in cool, dry conditions and avoid making more than your birds can consume while it remains fresh. Premixes and feeds may lose quality during prolonged storage, especially in hot weather. 

### 8. Can a Pearson Square create a complete poultry feed formula?

No. A Pearson Square normally balances one nutrient, usually crude protein, using two ingredients. It does not automatically balance energy, amino acids, vitamins, minerals, fiber, or feed cost.

## Conclusion

Successful poultry feed formulation is about more than mixing maize and soybean meal. You must match the feed to the bird’s age and purpose, balance energy and amino acids, supply appropriate calcium and phosphorus, use a reliable vitamin-mineral premix, and protect ingredients from mold and contamination.

Start with a clear nutrient target. Test or verify your ingredients, weigh everything accurately, mix micro-ingredients carefully, and monitor bird performance. Track body weight, feed intake, egg production, eggshell quality, mortality, litter condition, and feed wastage.

When the formula is based on sound nutrition and good farm management, your poultry receive the support they need for healthy growth, efficient production, and better profitability.
