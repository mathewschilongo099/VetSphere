Most lamb losses happen in the first days of life. Protecting your flock starts with strict routines in the lambing shed to prevent avoidable setbacks. Maintaining clean conditions and proper handling protects both ewes and lambs from infections right when they are most vulnerable.

## Wash Up and Suit Up Before You Touch a Ewe

Hand hygiene is critical during lambing to prevent infection and zoonotic disease transmission. Make it a strict habit to wash hands thoroughly before and after contact with each animal. Your everyday clothes and boots pick up pathogens from around the property and carry them straight into the nursery area. Clothing and boots should be managed specifically for the farm. Use farm-specific clothing and boots, regularly cleaned and disinfected, so you are not tracking outside bacteria into the pens where vulnerable newborns lie. Taking these basic sanitation steps before touching any animal stops diseases from passing between ewes during a busy lambing period.

## Keep Your Lambing Tools Sterile

![a small white lamb standing alone in a green grassy field](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1711648988212-0ad688a70e29?crop=entropy&cs=tinysrgb&fit=max&fm=jpg&ixid=M3w5ODE1NjV8MHwxfHNlYXJjaHwyfHxsYW1icyUyMG9uJTIwYSUyMGZhcm18ZW58MXwwfHx8MTc5NTU3MTI2N3ww&ixlib=rb-4.1.0&q=80&w=1080)


Assisting births or feeding young stock means your gear needs to be spotless. Equipment used during lambing must be kept sterile. Take the time to clean and sterilize all tools, including stomach tubes and lambing aids, before and after use. Leaving dirty equipment in the shed or rinsing it carelessly invites bacteria straight into the next animal. Scrubbing and sterilizing every tool protects the next ewe and lamb from secondary infections. Clean gear ensures that when you must step in to help a difficult birth or feed a weak newborn, you are not introducing harmful pathogens into a fresh system.

## Scrub and Disinfect the Pens Right

![several sheep and small lambs standing inside a barn pen with straw on the ground](https://images.pexels.com/photos/23630494/pexels-photo-23630494.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&h=650&w=940)


Clean physical spaces stop environmental diseases from building up in the shed. Livestock facility disinfectants can be used for cleaning lambing pens. Common choices include chlorhexidine, quaternary ammonium compounds, or a 10 percent bleach solution as noted by zubairkhalid.com. Always follow the product label instructions for proper dilution and contact time to make sure the product actually works. After disinfecting, let pens dry completely before adding fresh bedding. Putting dry straw over a wet, chemical-soaked floor ruins the effort and exposes newborns to damp conditions. Proper drying time ensures the disinfectant has done its job before new animals occupy the space.

## Handle Wet Lambs as Little as Possible

Newborns need time to bond with their mothers and dry off without human interference. Handling of wet lambs should be minimized. When intervention is necessary, aim to minimize the amount of handling of wet lambs and carry them suspended by their front legs with their backs towards you while keeping navel facing away. Carrying them this way prevents unnecessary stress and protects their delicate bodies during movement. Excessive touching or improper carrying can disrupt mothering up and cause accidental injury to a newborn animal that is still finding its feet.

## Manage Nursery Groups and Daily Checks

Once ewes and lambs leave the individual lambing pens, mixing them into larger groups too fast creates crowding and disease risks. Nursery pens should contain 5 to 7 ewes and their lambs whose families are usually less than 3 days old, as reported by MSU Extension Animal and Range Science in Montana. This specific group size keeps competition low and allows young families to thrive without undue stress or trampling risks. Stay vigilant by checking ewes and lambs at least twice a day to spot any health issues early. While the exact mortality rates or exact percentages of lamb losses in the first days of life are not detailed in the available text, keeping a close eye on your animals twice daily helps you catch fading lambs or postpartum issues in ewes before they escalate.

## What to Do This Week

Set up your farm-specific clothing and boots near the lambing area so you remember to change before entering the pens. Gather your stomach tubes, lambing aids, and tools, and establish a cleaning station to sterilize them before and after every use. Check your disinfectant supplies to ensure you have approved options like chlorhexidine, quaternary ammonium compounds, or a 10 percent bleach solution ready for pen cleaning. Plan to check your ewes and lambs at least twice a day, and keep your nursery groups limited to 5 to 7 ewes with lambs under 3 days old to maintain a clean, low-stress environment.
