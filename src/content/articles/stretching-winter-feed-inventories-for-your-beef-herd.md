## Count your bales before the snow sets in

![several black and white cows grazing in a wide green pasture](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1566310203664-bb0828838943?crop=entropy&cs=tinysrgb&fit=max&fm=jpg&ixid=M3w5ODE1NjV8MHwxfHNlYXJjaHwxfHxjb3dzJTIwZ3JhemluZyUyMHBhc3R1cmV8ZW58MXwwfHx8MTc5NTI2OTk3N3ww&ixlib=rb-4.1.0&q=80&w=1080)


Feed costs take up the biggest share of your budget. In fact, costs associated with feeding the producing beef cow represent over sixty percent of the total costs in a cow-calf production system. When you are looking at tight supplies or dry conditions, knowing exactly what is in the stack matters more than ever.

Take stock of your forage inventory right now and compare it with what the herd will actually need through the cold months. For planning purposes, a herd of twenty beef cows can easily consume 8 to 10, 4ft. x 4 ft. round bales per week. If your numbers do not match up, you need to know now rather than when the snow is knee-deep.

## Hitting the right body condition score

![several cattle standing in a grassy field under an open sky](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1498191923457-88552caeccb3?crop=entropy&cs=tinysrgb&fit=max&fm=jpg&ixid=M3w5ODE1NjV8MHwxfHNlYXJjaHwyfHxiZWVmJTIwY2F0dGxlfGVufDF8MHx8fDE3OTUyNjk5ODJ8MA&ixlib=rb-4.1.0&q=80&w=1080)


Winter weather puts extra demands on a cow's internal furnace. Mature beef cows in good condition with heavy winter hair coat during midgestation may adapt to a lower critical temperature (LCT) as low as minus 6 F when conditions stay dry and calm.

Keep an eye on flesh condition as the temperature drops. Aim for a body condition score (BCS) of 5 to 6 for mature cows and 6 for first-calf heifers on the 1 to 9 scale. Cows that slip below these targets will struggle when the real freeze hits.

## Balancing the ration without wasting money

![cows grazing in a field](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1665105519765-a0ec8e027100?crop=entropy&cs=tinysrgb&fit=max&fm=jpg&ixid=M3w5ODE1NjV8MHwxfHNlYXJjaHwzfHxjYWx2ZXMlMjBpbiUyMGElMjBmaWVsZHxlbnwxfDB8fHwxNzk1MjY5OTgzfDA&ixlib=rb-4.1.0&q=80&w=1080)


Low-quality roughage needs help if cows are going to maintain their weight. When you look at protein supplements, an inclusion level of 15 percent of the total crude protein (or 20 percent of the DIP) as NPN can be used without significantly jeopardizing livestock performance.

Do not guess at the numbers. Work closely with a nutritionist or utilize ration-balancing software programs, such as CowBytes, to balance supplementation with your forage base. It stops you from wasting expensive feed on rations that do not hit the mark.

## Meeting hidden nutritional needs

Energy and protein are only part of the puzzle when the pasture goes brown. Beef cows require 40,000 IU to 60,000 IU of vitamin A daily prior to calving. Supply 100 per cent of the vitamin requirements to the cattle during the entire winter feeding program so you do not run into deficiency issues down the road.

Specific local weather patterns and regional drought severity can shift your feed quality from year to year, so testing your actual hay is always a smart move.

## What to do this week

Walk the yard, check your feed bunks, and run the math on your remaining bales. Do not feed only by thumb rules; check if your cows clean up all the hay you feed them and adjust if they need more during cold weather. Act early if additional feed is needed to explore options, compare prices, and secure quality feed rather than settling for last-minute purchases. If you run into trouble balancing a tricky ration, ask a veterinarian or your local veterinary authority for guidance.
