## Check Your Roofs and Seal Up the Barns

Winter storms put a heavy strain on farm buildings. Take time now to inspect your livestock shelters and verify the roof structure can safely support heavy snow loads. Repair any existing leaks and install storm shutters, doors, and windows to keep freezing drafts out. These steps help prevent roof collapse and water intrusion during severe cold weather.

Fire safety is just as important when you close up the barns. Heat lamps, heaters, and electric cords must be certified, free of rodent damage, and kept at least 0.5 m from bedding. Always keep a functional fire extinguisher nearby and ensure an emergency evacuation plan is clearly understood by anyone working on the farm.

## Stock Up on Feed and Secure Your Water Supply

![Row of brown and white cows eating grass in a rustic barn setting.](https://images.pexels.com/photos/5216152/pexels-photo-5216152.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&h=650&w=940)


Feed supplies need a careful inventory well before temperatures drop. If water delivery fails in January, the margin for error is slim. Stock extra dry feed, specifically 20 to 30 percent more energy, to help cattle generate internal heat during bitter cold spells. Conducting an inventory of feed and emergency supplies now helps prevent shortages later in the season.

Water is often harder to manage than feed when a storm hits. Cattle cannot meet their daily water requirements by licking snow, and dehydration becomes a major hazard, especially when animals are on higher-fiber diets. Fit your water tanks or waterers with deicers or heaters to keep water flowing and reduce manual labor. Check water availability multiple times per day during a storm to ensure your herd stays properly hydrated.

## Beat the Wind Chill with Bedding and Windbreaks

![cows grazing in a field](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1665105519765-a0ec8e027100?crop=entropy&cs=tinysrgb&fit=max&fm=jpg&ixid=M3w5ODE1NjV8MHwxfHNlYXJjaHwzfHxjYWx2ZXMlMjBpbiUyMGElMjBmaWVsZHxlbnwxfDB8fHwxNzk1NDkxNjM1fDA&ixlib=rb-4.1.0&q=80&w=1080)


Low temperatures alone are rarely the only enemy your herd faces. Wind and moisture pose a much greater threat to cattle than low temperature alone.

Help your animals maintain their core condition by setting up sturdy windbreaks and providing deep, dry bedding. This setup helps cattle maintain condition below their lower critical temperature. Monitor your cattle closely for shivering or abnormal behavior. If you notice persistent issues, contact a licensed veterinarian promptly for guidance. Having supplies like a stomach tube and Bloatguard on hand can also help when specific digestive emergencies arise during cold weather.

## Put Together a Written Emergency Action Plan

When a storm knocks out the power, you need to move quickly. Well-maintained backup generators are essential for livestock production operations during winter storms, providing emergency or alternative heat sources when power outages or fuel shortages occur. Test your generator before bad weather arrives and keep enough fuel on hand.

Write down a clear emergency action plan that lists responsibilities for everyone on the farm. Post emergency procedures for loose livestock, injuries, extreme weather, and power outages in visible locations around the yards. Check your first-aid kits now and replace any expired items.

Include contact information for a licensed veterinarian in your emergency response plan, and make sure backup personnel know exactly who to call if health issues pop up during a storm. Conducting regular plan drills helps everyone move from reactive firefighting to proactive management, improving animal welfare and business continuity during extreme weather.

## Your Checklist for This Week

Walk your yards this week and check every water heater, backup generator, and fire extinguisher. Restock your first-aid kits and make sure the emergency phone numbers, including your vet's contact details, are posted clearly by the door. Verify your feed reserves and ensure extra dry feed is on hand.
